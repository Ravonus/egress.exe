# Discovery and critter codec

This expansion is in development. The public download remains the preceding verified bench release until the complete native, browser and release checks pass. Native asset checks and host recipe proofs do not establish main-game acceptance.

The catalogue has 444 stable nine-bit IDs: 384 materials (24 families with 16 forms), 24 bombs, 24 tools and 12 critters. The editable rules live in `assets/alchemy/catalogue.json`. Run `tools/bake_catalogue.py` to validate the graph and emit the native lookup tables and `build/catalogue/resolved.json`. All 444 discoveries have a finite recipe from FIRE, WATER, AIR, EARTH and METAL. There are 5,283 symmetric valid pairs. A failed mix consumes nothing; a successful mix consumes two units when the result is collected. Discovery ownership persists separately from carried quantities.

The bag holds 24 units, including duplicate individuals. MIX, BAG and LOG occupy separate native pages. LOG records discoveries and their canonical recipes; it does not grant materials or summon discovered critters for free. The shared fusion renderer deforms the actual two ingredient contours into the actual product. Its 1,126-byte paused SRAM workspace replaces separate animation payloads for individual recipes. Native 16×16 cells are doubled for the bench and reused as small inventory icons.

`assets/alchemy/colours.json` defines each family's target body colour and bounded hue/saturation/value variation. The baker quantizes 64 readable ramps per family to CGB RGB555. The run seed and discovery ID select a stable ramp; movement, facing and animation frames never reroll it. These are palette lookups, without runtime floating point or colour generation. Edit the targeted colours and rerun `tools/bake_catalogue_art.py`; `test/test_catalogue_art.py` checks native cell and contrast budgets.

Each critter has authored front, side, back and diagonal views in `assets/alchemy/pets/`. Five stored views mirror into eight facings. Every view has idle, follow, cast, rest, hurt, guard, heal and death poses. Feet, wings, hopping and crawling use the critter's gait. `assets/alchemy/companions.json` defines health, armour, role, automatic ability, command, stamina cost, cooldown and traits. Twelve profiles cover tanks, healers, attackers, damage over time, tactical debuffs and support buffs. Automatic abilities reserve stamina for the heir. SELECT commands the equipped critter; it never creates a new individual.

Ordinary critter death is permanent. Vine Frog alone currently carries `second_spring`: one self-revival at half health, with a spent flag belonging to that individual. It cannot faint or recover through ordinary healing. The bag retains each individual's health, spent charge, cooldown and unique instance ID. Paired CRC journals tie the individual records to the matching floor checkpoint. Death updates that matching checkpoint individual without copying newly collected ingredients onto an unmodified room. Duplicate species remain separate individuals.

Medicine heals the heir first, including a missing quarter heart. When the heir is completely healthy it heals a living critter. It remains on the ground when both are healthy, and cannot revive a dead critter. It does not obstruct crates, falling loads or living obstacles. No-combat puzzle proofs ignore medical decoration because health is outside that solver's state.

Crafted materials are finite uses. Bombs consume a crafted flask, spend stamina, travel with a visible fuse and react across a bounded area. Tools remain in the bag and recharge. Their effects use the canonical element/tile and conditional combat rules; failed targets keep the item. Bombs reuse the eight projectile slots, with at most one terrain reaction processed per simulation tick. Two health bodies and two small fuse-flask frames share twelve resident OBJ tiles; the companion uses two alternating four-tile buffers. All actor drawing uses the existing per-line OAM allocator.

The ordinary and streamed generators supply base reagents rather than prepared puzzle answers. Small rooms preflight their six local item records before placing optional locks. The accepted seed bank must be rebaked and solver-validated after a generator change. A room reset reconstructs the current floor; a new run gets a new seed, dimensions, shape and arrangement.

The expansion uses fresh game journals (meta 3, family 4, preferences/tutorial 3 and continue 3). Earlier game progress is rejected rather than migrated into the new generator and nine-bit inventory. Cartridge/host clock records retain their independent formats. Boot validation reads old records without rewriting them; a new game writes the new epoch.
